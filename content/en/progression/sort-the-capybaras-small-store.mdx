## Reading the Board Before You Move

The single biggest difference between players who sort the capybaras quickly and players who restart constantly is a five-second scan before the first move. Rushing the opening move is the most common way a clean board turns into a dead end.

- **Count your capybara types.** Knowing how many distinct groups exist tells you how crowded the board really is.
- **Find the empty slots first.** Free space is your most valuable resource, and it disappears fast.
- **Locate blockers or locked shelves.** Anything that limits movement should be cleared before you chase points.
- **Check the order queue.** A customer waiting on a specific capybara changes your priority order immediately.
- **Identify one escape route.** Always leave yourself at least one move that opens new space.

| Board element | What it usually signals | Best response |
|---|---|---|
| Matched group of three or more | A chance to free up slots | Clear it if it opens a lane |
| Lone capybara | Needs a partner to complete | Park it near a matching group |
| Blocked or locked shelf | Movement is restricted | Deal with it before scoring moves |
| Timed order card | Deadline pressure is coming | Serve it before casual sorting |
| Nearly full board | You are one bad move from a dead end | Use a booster now, not later |

### Early Boards: Build the Habit

In the opening stages, resist the urge to use boosters. The boards are forgiving enough that a careful player can clear them with basic matching, and every booster you save becomes insurance later. Focus on recognizing the shape of a complete set at a glance.

### Mid-Game: Order Rushes Change Everything

Once customers start queueing, your sorting decisions have two bosses. The trick is to treat order cards as a soft timer rather than a hard one — serve the closest order first, then return to clearing space. Players who try to serve every order at once usually finish neither task well.

### Late-Game: Efficiency Beats Speed

At this point the boards are tight and the timers are shorter. Efficiency means fewer wasted moves, not faster tapping. If you find yourself making three moves to accomplish what one move could do, stop and re-scan before continuing.

| Phase | Top priority | Common trap |
|---|---|---|
| Early | Learning patterns | Burning boosters out of impatience |
| Mid | Consistent upgrade progress | Over-investing in a single upgrade lane |
| Late | Move efficiency | Rushing without scanning the board |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the fastest way to sort the capybaras on a crowded board?**
Scan first, then clear blockers, then build complete sets. Speed comes from fewer wasted moves, not from moving faster. If you can plan your first three moves before making one, you will clear most crowded boards comfortably.

**Do I need to spend real money to progress?**
No. Progression in this style of puzzle is driven by pattern recognition and upgrade choices. Paid items speed things up, but patient players clear the same content by saving boosters and spending coins on permanent improvements.

**How do I handle timed orders without failing?**
Treat the queue as a soft timer. Serve the closest, easiest order first, then return to clearing space. Trying to satisfy every customer simultaneously is the fastest way to fail both the orders and the board.

**Is there a point where the small store stops getting harder?**
Difficulty tends to plateau rather than spike forever. Once your upgrades catch up with the board density, later stages feel more like a rhythm than a challenge — which is exactly when you should start chasing higher star ratings.
