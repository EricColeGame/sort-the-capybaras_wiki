## Keyboard Shortcuts, Remapping, and Accessibility

Keyboard players get the most granular control, because hotkeys skip the cursor entirely. The shortcuts below reflect the conventions most builds follow, but confirm them in your own settings menu before you build muscle memory around them.

| Shortcut | Typical action | When it helps most |
|---|---|---|
| Arrow keys / WASD | Move the selection cursor | Precise placement in dense grids |
| Tab | Cycle between stacks or lanes | When several capybaras overlap |
| Enter / Space | Confirm the highlighted slot | Speed rounds where dragging wastes time |
| Ctrl+Z or U | Undo the last move | Recovering from a misdrop |
| H | Reveal a hint | Learning an unfamiliar layout |
| R | Restart the round | Practicing one specific board |
| Esc / P | Pause | Catching your breath in timed modes |
| M | Mute or unmute | Late-night sessions |

Most builds also let you rebind keys, and it is worth doing. Put undo on a key your left hand rests on naturally, and keep restart away from the keys you use constantly so you do not reset a good run by accident.

Accessibility settings are part of the control scheme too, not a separate concern. Look for a larger cursor, high-contrast outlines on draggable capybaras, reduced motion for background animations, and a hold-versus-toggle option for dragging. If you want a broader reference for what good control accessibility looks like across games, the [Xbox Accessibility Guidelines](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/guidelines) cover remapping, motor, and cognitive considerations in plain language.

### Pro Tips for Faster Sorting

- **Choose one input and commit.** Switching weekly resets your muscle memory.
- **Prefer select-then-place when the grid is tight.** It removes the drop-accuracy problem entirely.
- **Keep undo within one keypress.** Fast recovery matters more than perfect play.
- **Zoom in if targets are small.** A larger capybara is an easier capybara to grab.
- **Play a few untimed rounds first.** Learn the board layout before you race the clock.
- **Go full-screen on mobile browsers.** It prevents page scrolling from stealing your swipes.
- **Rebind one key at a time.** Change everything at once and you will not know what helped.

## Troubleshooting Sort the Capybaras Controls That Stop Responding

When the sort the capybaras controls suddenly feel broken, the cause is usually mundane. Work through this table before you assume the game is bugged.

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Clicks and keys do nothing | The game window is not focused | Click once inside the play area |
| Capybaras land in the wrong slot | Dragging too fast with a large cursor offset | Slow down, or switch to select-then-place |
| Swiping scrolls the page instead | Browser gesture conflict | Enter full-screen mode |
| Controller is not detected | Browsers often register a gamepad only after a button press | Connect it, then press any button |
| Input feels delayed | Background tabs and apps competing for resources | Close other tabs and overlays |
| Hotkeys stopped working | A text field has focus, or bindings changed | Click the canvas and recheck settings |
| Drag will not release | Button held past the drop zone | Release earlier, or use tap-to-place |

If none of these resolve it, reset the control settings to default and test again. That single step separates a misconfigured binding from a genuine technical problem, and it takes less than a minute.

One last note on difficulty: many players assume harder rounds require faster hands. In practice, harder rounds require cleaner inputs. A player who never misdrops will beat a faster player who misdrops twice, every time.

## FAQ

**Can I change the sort the capybaras controls?**

In most builds, yes. Look for a settings or options menu with a controls or key bindings section. Rebind one action at a time, then test it in an untimed round before you commit to a full session.

**Are the controls different on mobile?**

They are, mainly because touch replaces the cursor. Tap-to-select and drag-to-place both usually exist on mobile, and tapping tends to be more accurate on small screens while dragging is quicker once you are comfortable.

**Why do my inputs feel delayed?**

Latency usually comes from the environment rather than the game. Close background tabs and apps, disable browser overlays, and make sure the game window has focus. On mobile, low-power mode can also throttle input responsiveness.

**Do I need a controller to play well?**

No. Mouse and keyboard remain the fastest option for most players, and touch works well for short sessions. A gamepad is a comfort choice rather than a competitive one, though it is a great fit for long couch sessions.
