## Troubleshooting When Sort the Capybaras Controller Support Stops Working

Most input failures fall into a handful of buckets. Match your symptom to the fix below.

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Pad never detected | Connected after launch | Reconnect, then relaunch the game |
| Cursor moves, buttons do nothing | Partial or wrong template | Switch to a different gamepad template |
| Every press registers twice | Two input layers active at once | Disable either the in-game option or the platform layer, not both |
| Cursor drifts on its own | Deadzone set too tight | Raise the stick deadzone in your remapper |
| Works in menus, dead in levels | Overlay stealing focus | Close overlays, alt-tab away and back |
| Noticeable input lag | Bluetooth interference | Switch to wired or 2.4 GHz |
| Wrong button prompts shown | Template doesn't match your pad brand | Pick the brand-matched template |

A quick sanity check before you change anything: open a [browser-based controller tester](https://gamepad-tester.com/) and confirm every button reports correctly outside the game. If a button is dead there too, the problem is hardware, not software. Community reports consistently show that "broken" controllers are usually just a low battery or a loose cable.

## Steam Deck, Big Picture, and Remapping Tools

On handheld PCs and in Big Picture mode, the platform's built-in input layer does the heavy lifting. It maps your pad to keyboard and mouse inputs, which is why games without native gamepad code still respond to a controller. Steam's own [Steam Input documentation](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/steam_controller) explains how templates, action sets, and community layouts work — worth reading if you want to build a custom layout.

On Windows, third-party remappers fill the same role for non-Steam builds. Common options include DS4Windows for PlayStation pads, the Xbox Accessories app for Microsoft hardware, reWASD for deep customization, and JoyToKey for pure keyboard emulation. Pick one tool, not three — stacked remappers are the number-one cause of double inputs.

## Tips for Sorting Faster With a Gamepad

Once sort the capybaras controller support is working in your build, small habits close most of the speed gap with a mouse.

- **Use the D-pad for single-step movement.** Sticks overshoot on tight grids.
- **Remap undo to a shoulder button** so it never leaves your grip.
- **Slow the cursor down, don't speed it up,** until your accuracy stabilizes.
- **Plan two moves ahead** so you're not dragging across the whole board.
- **Practice on easy levels** with the pad before tackling a dense board.
- **Look for a hold-versus-toggle option** for pick-up actions.
- **Keep the pad wired** during long sessions to avoid mid-puzzle dropouts.

None of these are dramatic changes, but together they make a gamepad feel less like a compromise and more like a preference.

## FAQ

**Does sort the capybaras controller support work on phones?**
Usually not natively. Most mobile builds are touch-first, and a Bluetooth pad will typically behave like a finger rather than a gamepad. Some players report success with their device's own screen-mapping tools, but results vary by phone and build.

**Can I remap the buttons?**
If the game offers a controls menu, yes. If it doesn't, use your platform's input layer or a desktop remapper to build a layout. That's the most reliable route for builds without in-game remapping.

**Why does my controller work in menus but not in the puzzle?**
This is almost always a focus or overlay problem. A notification overlay or a background app can steal input focus, so the game receives menu navigation but not in-level actions. Close overlays and re-enter the level.

**Is a controller actually better for this game?**
For short sessions, a mouse is faster. For long, relaxed sessions, a gamepad is more comfortable and keeps your posture stable. If sort the capybaras controller support works in your build, try it for a few levels before deciding.
