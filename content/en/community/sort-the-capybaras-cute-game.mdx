## Core Mechanics: How Sorting Actually Works

### A Typical Round, Step by Step

1. **Read the board.** Count how many distinct capybara types are visible before you touch anything.
2. **Open your slots.** Move the easiest matches first so your holding area stays flexible.
3. **Group by type.** Keep similar capybaras clustered so you can release them in one clean sweep.
4. **Watch the queue.** New pieces usually arrive in a predictable order — use that to plan two moves ahead.
5. **Spend boosters late.** A free swap is worth far more when only a few pieces remain.
6. **Clear the board.** Finishing with empty holding slots is the cleanest way to end a level.
7. **Reset your mindset.** If a board goes sideways, restarting is faster than untangling it.

The difficulty curve in most versions is gentle by design. Early stages teach one idea at a time, then later stages combine two or three ideas at once. According to player experience, the jump in challenge feels fair rather than sudden, which keeps the relaxing tone intact.

## Tips and Strategies From Player Experience

Regular players tend to converge on the same handful of habits. None of them require fast reflexes — they require attention.

- **Scan before you tap.** Ten seconds of looking saves a minute of fixing.
- **Work the edges first.** Freeing outer slots gives you room to maneuver later.
- **Bank matching pairs.** Holding two of a kind until a third appears is often better than placing early.
- **Sort by shape, not just color.** Patterns stay readable when the board gets busy.
- **Leave one slot empty.** A permanently open slot is your escape hatch.
- **Stop when you are tired.** Sorting rewards focus, and tired tapping creates messes.

| Tip | How to do it | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scan first | Survey the whole board before moving | Avoiding dead ends |
| Edge clearing | Empty outer slots before the center | Tight boards |
| Pair banking | Hold two of a kind, wait for a third | Long levels |
| Pattern sorting | Track shape and accessory, not only color | Visually busy stages |
| Booster patience | Save helpers for a genuine lock-up | Tricky late stages |
| Session pacing | Play in short, focused bursts | Consistency |

One more habit worth building: narrate the board to yourself. Saying "two blue, one striped" out loud sounds silly, but it forces your brain to hold the pattern in working memory instead of relying on a quick glance.

Most frustration in the sort the capybaras cute game comes from a small set of avoidable errors. Fixing them usually feels like unlocking a difficulty setting.

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Better habit |
