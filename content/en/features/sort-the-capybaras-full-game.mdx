## Tips and Strategies for Sorting Faster

Good sorting play comes down to a handful of repeatable habits. None of them require fast reflexes — they require discipline.

- **Scan before you sort.** Spend a few seconds mapping where each piece type lives before making a move.
- **Clear the biggest group first.** Removing the most crowded set of pieces frees the most slots.
- **Protect your empty slots.** An empty slot is a resource. Treat it like one.
- **Solve the blockers early.** If a locked piece is in the way, figure out what unlocks it before anything else.
- **Save boosters for combo boards.** Using a power-up on a simple board is usually a waste.
- **Restart early, not late.** If the board state is clearly lost, reset and try a different opening sequence.

| Strategy | What to do | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-scanning | Identify all matching pairs before tapping | Prevents the dead-end moves that cause most failures |
| Slot budgeting | Keep at least one slot free at all times | Gives you a buffer when the board gets tight |
| Blocker-first ordering | Clear whatever is blocking the most pieces | Unlocks more options per move |
| Group stacking | Build groups in a consistent order | Reduces the mental load on crowded boards |
| Deliberate restarting | Reset a losing board without hesitation | Saves time compared to grinding out a dead position |

It also helps to know the mistakes that quietly cost players levels. These come up constantly in player experience discussions.

| Common mistake | What it looks like | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Tapping reactively | Moving the first piece you notice | Pause and scan first |

| Ignoring blockers | Sorting around a locked piece for ten moves | Deal with the blocker immediately |
| Wasting boosters | Using a power-up on an easy board | Save them for layered obstacle levels |
| Playing too fast on timed boards | Rushing and creating dead ends | Prioritize clean clears over raw speed |
